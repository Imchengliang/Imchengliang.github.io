[E03f69e6e71] section=ABSTRACT
The research reports a novel green method to use citrus fruits for the management of spent NMC based lithium ion batteries (LIBs). Citrus fruit juice (CJ) can provide an excellent chemical combination to remove the binder and support the leaching with efficiency in between 94% to 100%. CJ have many advantages in LIBs recycling as an economic and green method due to rich in many organic acids like citric and malic acid as complexing agents with ascorbic acid and citrus flavonoids, for the reduction of many heavy metals. Application of CJ can avoid the use of N-Methylpyrrolidine, c-Butyrolactone, dimethylformamide, and dimethyl sulfoxide like toxic solvents commonly used for peeling off Al/Cu. Furthermore, counterions (like Na+, Mg ^+, Ca ^2+) present in CJ was responsible for the improvement in the leaching efficiency of organic acids. A mechanistic pattern of the overall reaction was also proposed and duly supported by various spectroscopic techniques. Binder removal experiment was supported by analytical techniques like XRD, XRF, IR, and FE-SEM, while the metal concentration was monitored by using ICP-MS analysis.  2016 Elsevier Ltd. [E1ac960baae] section=2.1. Binder removal and leaching
Binder removal experiments were carried out in batch mode using the 250 mL round-bottom flask with a temperature controlling facility. The flask was equipped with a magnetic stirrer for homogenization. Experiments were performed by using CJ with cathode and anode film. Optimum w/v ratio of film to CJ was found to be 50,000 mg/L at 90 ^∘C and 20 min for binder removal. Cu (anode) and Al (cathode) film were separate, washed with water and check its purity using XRD and XRF techniques. After the removal of Cu and Al the remaining solution was refluxed for 30 min and then filter to remove carbon and other impurities. The filtrate was used to check the leaching rate, using Eq. (2.1). Leaching rate is the percentage ratio of the amount of metal species in the leachate to the total amount, as measured by tri-acid digestion process (Uppsala, 2015) using ICP-MS.
