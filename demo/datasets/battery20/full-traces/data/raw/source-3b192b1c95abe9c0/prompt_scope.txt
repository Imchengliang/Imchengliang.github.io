[E14659bfd7d] section=ABSTRACT
To reduce cost and secondary pollution ofspent lithium ion battery (LIB) recycling caused by complicated separation and purification, a novel simplified recycling process is investigated in this paper. Removal of magnesium is a common issue in hydrometallurgy process. Considering magnesium as an important additive in LIB modification, tolerant level of magnesium in leachate is explored as well. Based on the novel recycling technology, Li [ (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3)1-x Mgx ] O2 (0 ≤x ≤0.05) cathode materials are achieved from spent LIB. Tests ofXRD, SEM, TG-DTA and so on are carried out to evaluate material properties. Electrochemical test shows an initial charge and discharge capacity ofthe regenerated LiNi1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3 O2 to be 175.4 mAh g−1 and 152.7 mAh g ^1 (2.7 – 4.3, 0.2 C),, respectively. The capacity remains 94% ofthe original value after 50 cycles (2.7–4.3 V, 1 C). Results indicate that presence of magnesium up to x = 0.01 has no significant impact on overall performance of Li [ (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3)1 Mg ]O. As a result, magnesium level as high as 360 mg L−1 in leachate remains tolerable. Compared with conventional limitation ofmagnesium content, the elimination level of magnesium exceeded general impurity-removal requirement.

For Lee [7], LiCoO2 was separated from spent electrodes in a single synthetic step using hydrothermal method without any scraping procedure, and the renovated LiCoO2 cathode material was obtained simultaneously. Nan et al. [8] achieved LiCoO2 cathode material with good electrochemical performance which was synthesized by recovered compounds of cobalt and lithium.

The whole recycling route comprises the main steps schematically shown in Fig. 1: preliminary treatment of batteries, followed by leaching with alkaline and sulfuric acid solution respectively, metal separation by liquid–liquid extraction and chemical deposition method and synthesis ofcathode active materials. As indicated in Fig. 1, concentration ofthe purified solution is adjusted by adding an appropriate amount of corresponding metal salts or dilution with distilled water to 11.5 mol L ^-1 mix aqueous solution of NiSO4, CoSO4, MnSO4 (Ni:Co:Ni cationic ratio of 1:1:1). The above regenerated solution is used as starting materials, a 1.5 mol L ^-1 Na2CO3 aqueous solution as precipitant, and NH3H2O as the chelating agent. These aqueous solutions are dripped simultaneously into a three mouth flask using a two-channel peristaltic pump, in which a certain amount of NH3H2O is added in advance as a buffer, and reaction system is maintained at 55 ^∘𝖢 with continuous stirring. The pH value of the mixture is regulated to 7.5–8.0 and related operating parameters are controlled carefully as well. This leads to the precipitation of Ni, Co and Mn carbonate with a homogeneous cation distribution. The (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3) CO3 precipitates are separated by filtration after aging, and washed with distilled water several times until the absence of BaSO4 precipitate by BaCl addition. The metal carbonate is then dried at 80 ^∘C overnight in vacuum. The (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3) CO3 precursor is calcined initially at 550 ^∘C for 5 h in air. The pre-calcined complex oxide is then mixed sufficiently with Li2CO3 salt at a Li/M molar ratio of 1.05 using an agate mortar, and finally sintered in air at 920 ^∘C for 15 h to obtain the cathode material.

[Ee9130f424e] section=3.5. Electrochemical performances
Figs. 10 and 11 show the first charge/discharge curves and the corresponding capacity vs. cycle number curves of all the Li [ (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3)1-x Mgx ] O2 (x = 0, 0.01, 0.025, 0.05) cells in the voltage range between 2.7 and 4.3 V at 2 C current rate (300 m Ag ^-1), while Figs. 12 and 13 demonstrate the electrochemical performance cycled between 2.7 and 4.5 V at 0.5 C current rate. It is revealed that the introduction of impurity magnesium ion in Li [ (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3)1-x Mgx ] O2 affects the elec trochemical performance primarily by a reduction in capacity but cycle performance retained well [23–27]. As can be seen, while cells are cycled at high current density or between high voltage range, although the capacity loss is obvious with the increasing of magnesium content, capacity retention rate is improved by trace magnesium presence. In Li [ (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3)1-x Mgx ] O2 cathode materials, the magnesium ions cannot be oxidized during cell charge, namely, electrochemically inactive. Magnesium cations, with a radius close to that of lithium, might move to the interslab position during Li ^+ de-intercalation and act as pillar which in turn assist the migration of Li ^+ ion and suppress the cation disorder. The presence ofan optimum concentration ofmagnesium ions prevents any local collapse of the inter-slab space upon repeated charge–discharge cycling [21,22]. Therefore, magnesium doping in layered Li [ Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3 ] O2 improves the structure stability and enhanced cycling stability is observed. Enhancement in cycling performance can be achieved when the amount of magnesium is less than 0.01 (x < 0.01). However, both the reversible capacity and capacity retention rate decrease at high magnesium ion level (x > 0.025).

[E322135fa6e] section=3.5. Electrochemical performances
Variable valence of impurity ions in leaching solution is often not available, which means that those impurity ions are always electrochemically inactive. Presence of Mg ^ 2̅ + in purified leaching solution decreases the active species of the as-prepared cathode material. Thereby, the higher the magnesium content is, the lower the capacity of the electrode. There is a declining trend in electrochemical performance while the addition ofMg is beyond the molar percentage of 0.025 (x > 0.025), namely, magnesium ion might be detrimental at such high level.

[E774c888016] section=3.5. Electrochemical performances
As can be implied from the measurements of XRD, SEM and electrochemical properties, low content of Mg in Li [ (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3)1-x Mgx ] O2 (x ≤0.01) cathode materials has no significant impact on the overall performance of the electrode. Economic profits of the recovered product with acceptable magnesium impurity presence are satisfactory given the savings of purification expense. Once converted into the concentration of magnesium in purified solution used for preparation of carbonate precursor, the elimination level of magnesium ion can be enlarged to 360 mgL ^-1, far from the preliminary 40 m g L ^-1. This value greatly exceeds the general level required by impurity removal, preventing from the loss of main valuable metals caused by long and complicated purification process, which in turn cut down disposal cost dramatically.

(3) XRD showed that Mg successfully replaced Co within the layered structure. All of the Li [ (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3)1-x Mgx ] O2 (0 ≤x ≤0.05) samples were found to be spherical particle. Over the voltage range 2.7–4.3 V at constant current 2 C rate, or between the 2.7–4.5 V at 0.5 C rate, electrochemical research revealed that the reversible capacities of the Li [ (Ni1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3)1-x Mgx ] O2 (0 ≤x ≤0.05) cells decreased with the increasing Mg ^2+ content but the presence of Mg was found beneficial in enhancing the cycling performance at low content (x ≤0.01), however, high content of Mg (x ≥0.025) led to evident capacity loss and decline of cyclability because homogeneous distribution of Mg ^2+ ions in the material became difficult at high content with the present method.
