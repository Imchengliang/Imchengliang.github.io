Introduction Lithium ion batteries (LIBs) are extensively used as electrochemical power sources for portable electronic devices due to their outstanding electrochemical performances. With the development of these portable electronic products, the worldwide consumption of LIBs has increased tremendously. Total worldwide rechargeable batteries production is estimated to be 7.0 billion in 2015 [1]. Therefore, it is expected that a massive amount of exhausted batteries together with waste generated during LIB manufacturing process will become a focus issue. Spent LIBs contain large amounts of valuable metals; meanwhile, they are reorganized as typical hazardous solid waste due to toxic heavy metals and corrosive electrolytes. Nowadays, highly concerns on environmental preserve as well as reuse of secondary resource demand for desirable disposal ofbattery waste. Moreover, strict environmental legislation and regulations stimulate the recycling for batteries waste. Consequently, clean recycling of spent Lithium ion batteries (LIBs) is a critical issue to achieve a sustainable LIB industry. Lithium cobalt oxide is the first commercial cathode material available in the market. Lots of research has been carried out to recover metal values such as cobalt and lithium from LiCoO2 active materials [1–4]. The current recycling processes for spent lithiumion batteries have been reviewed by Xu et al. [4]. The overall recycling technology basically consists of physical and chemical processes. The majority chemical processes employ dilute HCl, HNO3, H2SO4, or even mixed acid to dissolve the cathode material [5]. Co, Li and Cu are easily leached from spent Li-ion batteries using acid solutions and reducing agents like hydrogen peroxide (H2O2). H2O2 is usually added in order to convert all cobalt or manganese to the 2+ state which was more readily dissolvable in acid solution. Solvent extraction, precipitation method, crystallization, ion exchange and electrolysis were found as main separation operations proposed to treat leaching liquors [3,6], while solvent extraction using cationic commercial extractants such as D2EHPA, PC-88A, Cyanex 272 and Acorga M5640 to separate metal values presented very promising results. In addition, using spent batteries to prepare LiCoO2 electrode materials was also investigated. For Lee [7], LiCoO2 was separated from spent electrodes in a single synthetic step using hydrothermal method without any scraping procedure, and the renovated LiCoO2 cathode material was obtained simultaneously. Nan et al. [8] achieved LiCoO2 cathode material with good electrochemical performance which was synthesized by recovered compounds of cobalt and lithium. Nevertheless, traditional LiC ρ̅ _ o O _ 2 cathode material has disadvantages of high cost and inferior stability. Scientists and engineers have worked on searching for alternative materials [9–11]. Research and development have been made to synthesize cathode materials such as Li (Ni1-x-y Cox Mny) 02, LiMn2O4,, and LiFePO4 with enhanced performance and environmentally more friendly [12,13]. Among them, the layered LiNi1 / 3 Co1 / 3 Mn1 / 3 O2 has been widely used because ofits excellent performance [ 14, 15 ]. Attention has been paid to recover and recycle Li (Ni1-x-y Cox Mny) O2 cathode materials for they contain rich valuable metals. Up to now, most of the research works in the chemical process cannot fulfill the complete separation ofeach metal value from the Li (Ni1-x-y Cox Mny) O2 cathode materials. Wang et al. [16] developed a recycling process to separate and recover as much of the valuable metals as possible.
