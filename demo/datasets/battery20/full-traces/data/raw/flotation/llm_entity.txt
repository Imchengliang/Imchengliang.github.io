The TG results are shown in Figure 3, and pyrolysis products at pyrolysis temperatures of 120 and 500 ^∘ that correspond to the weight loss peaks in the DTG curve are present in Figure 4. The first weight loss stage appears when pyrolysis temperature ranges from 30 to 150 ^∘C; this is mainly caused by the volatilization of residual electrolyte. The decomposition of organic binder causes the second weight loss during the temperature of 450 to 550 ^∘C.. Similar TG and DTG curves of LiCoO2 and graphite demonstrate that the LiCoO2 and graphite used in this study have the same organic binder of PVDF. The DTG analysis shows the maximum weight loss appears at 500 ^∘C, and it indicates that pyrolysis can make organic binders fully decompose at 500 ^∘Ċ. Therefore, the pyrolysis experiments are conducted at a temperature of 500 ^∘C On the basis of the TG analysis, the pyrolysis products of electrode materials at temperatures of 120 and 500 ^∘C were sampled via the auto injector system that was connected with the GC and TGA system, and gas analysis was conducted in the GC/MS system. Figure 4 demonstrates that pyrolysis products at a pyrolysis temperature of 120 ^∘C come from ester electrolyte, such as dimethyl carbonate, ethylene carbonate, and propylene carbonate. Organic pyrolysis products at a pyrolysis temperature of 500 ^∘C are mostly fluorides that mainly come from the decomposition of PVDF, such as vinylidene fluoride, 1,3,5-trifluorobenzene, 1,4-difluoroben zene, ^1, 1, 1, 3, 3, 3 -hexafluoro-propane, and 1,2,4-trifluoroben zene, which demonstrates that PVDF has been decomposed at a pyrolysis temperature of 500 ^∘C. In the pyrolysis process, a part of the polymer structure o - (CH2 - CF2) - n decomposes to a single molecule of CH2 = CF2 while others separate out HF and their carbon chain fracture to form small molecules. Ethylene carbonate and propylene carbonate are from residual electrolyte. These results demonstrate that the main organics in electrode materials are organic binder and electrolyte and pyrolysis can effectively remove organic binder and electrolyte. Effects of Pyrolysis on the Physicochemical Properties of Electrode Materials. After the crushing and sieving processes, phase compositions of the raw electrode materials and pyrolytic electrode materials with a size of −0.075 mm were tested by XRD, and their results were shown in Figure 5. No new material peaks are detected by XRD in LiCoO or graphite, which indicates that crystal structures of LiCoO2 or graphite are not changed at a pyrolysis temperature of 500 ^∘C. At a pyrolysis temperature of 5̇ 00 ^∘C, only decomposition of organic binders happens on the surface of the electrode material particles, and it does not influence the physicochem ical properties of electrode materials; this is an advantage for the subsequent separation process.
