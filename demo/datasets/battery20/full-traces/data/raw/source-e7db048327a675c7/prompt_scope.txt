[E0e114227b8] section=ABSTRACT
Reductive leaching of LiCoO2 from spent lithium ion battery was investigated in terms of reaction variables. The leaching efficiency of LiCoO2 increased with increasing temperature, and concentration of HNO3, but with decreasing solid/liquid (S/L) ratio. Li and Co from LiCoO were leached over 95% with the addition of 1.7 vol Ω^0 / ΩH2 O2 as a reducing agent. This is due to the reduction of Co ^3+ to Co ^2+ which can be readily dissolved. The dissolution rates of Co and Li were inversely proportional to their respective concentrations in the solution. Apparent activation energies of 12.5 and 11.4 kcal/mol were obtained for Co and Li, respectively. This indicates that the dissolution of LiCoO2 is controlled by surface chemical reaction. [E247d73fe99] section=1. Introduction
Recycling of lithium batteries has become important because, due to their characteristics of lightness and good performance (Lundblad and Bergman, 1997; Plichta and Salomon, 1987), these batteries are increasingly substituting for other batteries. In 2000, the worldwide production of lithium ion secondary batteries (LIBs) reached about 500 millions cells of which Korea has a 10% market share. From this consumption, LIB waste is annually estimated at 200 to 500 MT, with a metal content of 5 to 15 wt.% Co and 2 to 7 wt.% Li.

[Ee9d179bba7] section=1. Introduction
Spent lithium primary batteries cannot be disposed of unless the contained metallic lithium is properly removed, due to its explosive nature. Also, lithium cobalt oxide is adopted as cathodic active material; therefore, valuable metals such as cobalt and lithium have to be recycled (Sabin, 1997; Shackle and Calif, 1994; Suita, 1990; William et al., 1982; Zhang et al., 1998). Thus, there are two problems to be solved; disposal of harmful waste and prevention of explosion during recycling of LIB waste. Metallic lithium in the used LIBs can be formed on graphite anode by overcharging and abnormal deposition. Vigorous oxidation of metallic lithium with moisture can be dangerous during the mechanical treatment of spent LIBs. For successful recovery of cobalt and lithium from spent LIBs, safety in mechanical treatment, and waste minimization are considered to be the most important.

[E5b46903108] section=1. Introduction
In this study, acid leaching has been applied to recover cobalt and lithium from cathodic active material of spent LIB. The cathodic active material was separated by physical processes involving thermal treatment, shredding, and vibrating screening as shown in Fig. 1 (Lee and Rhee, 2002). Thermal pretreatment was necessary for the separation of cell units from the battery package. However, it caused thermal hardening of the plastic casing, and partial oxidation of metallic lithium. Thus, the batteries were shredded for the liberation of electrode materials; a shredder was designed considering the selectivity of active materials as well as the reduction of cost and impurities (Lee and Rhee, 2000).

[Ef908ed912e] section=1. Introduction
Reductive leaching of the cathodic active material with H2O2 addition in a nitric acid solution was employed for a better dissolution of cobalt and lithium. Effects of temperature, HNO3 concentration, solid/liquid (S/L) ratio, and H2O2 concentration were investigated. The kinetics of the reductive leaching was also studied.

[E7ec1a05529] section=2. Experimental procedures
Cathodic active material from lithium ion secondary battery was separated by a series of thermal treatment, high-speed shredding, calcination steps (Lee and Rhee, 2002). First, LIB samples were thermally treated in a muffle furnace at 150 ^∘C for 1 h. Then, the samples were disassembled with a highspeed shredder. Separated segments were simultaneously classified into the range 1–10 mm in size. Second, thermal treatment was performed in a furnace, and electrode materials were separated from the current collectors by vibrating screening. Next, cathodic active material, LiCoO2 was obtained by burning carbon and binder off in the temperature range 700– 900 ^∘C for 1 h. Fig. 2 shows XRD patterns of calcined cathodic active material at temperature of 700– 900 ^∘C.. The diffractogram indicates that the carbon and binder were burnt off by calcining above 800 ^∘C.. The cathodic active material was ground in a ball mill and dried in an oven at 60 ^∘C for 48 h. The mean particle size was about 15 Am, as shown in Fig. 3 (Lee and Rhee, 2002).

1. By reductive leaching with the addition of hydrogen peroxide as a reducing agent, the leaching efficiency increased by 45% for Co and 10% for Li compared with that in only nitric acid leaching. This behavior seems to be due to the reduction of Co^3 + to Co2 +, which is readily dissolved. The leaching efficiency of Co and Li increased with increasing HNO3 concentration, temperature, and hydrogen peroxide concentration and with decreasing S/L ratio. An effective condition for the leaching would be 1 M HNO3, 10 to 20 g/L initial S/L ratio, 75 ^∘C temperature, and 1.7 vol.% H2O2 addition.
