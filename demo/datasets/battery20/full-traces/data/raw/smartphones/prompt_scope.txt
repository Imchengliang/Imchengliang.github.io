[Ebc0ee7c03c] section=ABSTRACT
Although several recycling solutions are already being applied for electronic scraps, the seek for more efficient routes is a hotspot in research, and crucial to achieve the so needed circular economy. This study is the first step of a project that aims to develop recycling solutions for entire smartphones. A comprehensive characterization of all smartphones’ components and a definition of the main material targets for recovery are provided. Characterization included 30 devices of different brands fabricated before and after 2015, analysis of 50 metal elements, and FTIR analysis and loss on ignition for polymers. Significant information about tendencies of composition and distribution of materials were noted, such as the increasing in concentration of Al, and the decreasing in precious metals. The definition of material targets included parameters of mass fraction, economic potential, scarcity, and associated environmental burden (values are given for each material in all components). At the end, Cu, Al, Au, Pd, Co, and Li were found the most relevant elements for recovering. By addressing these elements (73% of the total mass of metals), 99% of the total economic potential could be recovered, as well as 90 and 97% of the calculated scarcity and environmental burden parameters, respectively.

Keywords Characterization · Circular economy · E-waste · WEEE · Battery

[Eba438898aa] section=Introduction
Smartphones play an important role in modern society, useful for daily activities, business, entertainment, and communication in general. Through their incredible practicability, smartphones can save time and money for the owners. Thus, the aforementioned advantages generate a high demand for this device, practically burying old feature phones. Globally, approximately 80% of the population own a smartphone [1], and the consumption is expected to increase, as new features, applicability, and materials are being developed, and lifespan decreases [2, 3].

[E26b832effa] section=Introduction
Since consumption generates wastes, the generation of end-of-life (EoL) smartphones also tends to increase [4]. In order to reach circular economy and sustainable patterns of consumption (which are mentioned as part of the sustainable development goals of the 2030 Agenda of the United Nations [5]), recycling strategies for electronic scraps became a recent hotspot in academic research. Proposed solutions normally focus on the separation and recovery of valuable components and materials, which can drive and support the recycling initiatives due to the economic and strategic relevance [6]. Smartphones are considered one of the most valuable e-wastes [7]. A non-exhaustive list of elements that can be found in smartphones is gold, silver, copper, palladium, tin, lithium, aluminum, indium, gallium, and cobalt [4, 8].

Based on the experience of the authors in dealing with e-waste recycling, one of the main benefits of conducting a proper characterization study is to gather data to define the target components and materials for the recycling route, as well as predict the viability of the routes. The authors manifest concern about the procedures of disassembling and segregating components from e-wastes (normally carried out in the first stage recycling after collection), in which recyclers do not have correct information about the composition of the residue, and often waste some valuable pieces. Recyclers are autonomous and not standardized in deciding what to do with the waste [12]. [E44f8f99f6a] section=Environmental burden as global warming potential
As recycling has an evident environmental appeal, a criterion for selecting environmental relevant materials is wanted. Each material has an environmental burden (E Bm) associated with it, which is generated through its entire life cycle (usually called “from cradle to grave” impacts, and normally quantified by life cycle analysis (LCA) tools) [34]. Recycling initiatives can potentially minimize the environmental burden of a given material by substituting the processes used in the primary ores treatment (mining, refining, and production of the crude material, usually called “from cradle to gate”) for the process applied to the secondary ores treatment (which can be very variable) [35, 36]. Thus, materials that contain a high environmental burden associated with the cradle to gate approach are especially wanted for recycling processes. Considering the steps after “gate” into the quantification of the environmental burden would not be appropriated because recycled materials can be applied in the production of any consumer good, and the impacts of these steps themselves would remain the same despite of the origin of the resources (primary or secondary sources).

[E25849b0f3b] section=Mass fraction
The definition of target components and materials by the parameter of mass fraction is a direct interpretation of the characterization results. The following discussion is based on the information provided in Tables 2 and 3.

[Ed5cde76cf2] section=Mass fraction
In the component casings and screens, Al is the most significant element (more than 50%). In batteries, Co is the element with the highest mass fraction, which corresponds to 46.1% of the total metals. Also considering the Al of the batteries, the amount of Al is increased to 72.5% of the total metals. In PCBs, Cu is the main element (54%). Additionally, Cu is the third element, in mass relevance, for casings, screens, and batteries. Besides Cu, Co, and Al, Fe is also relevant, as the second most significant for casings, screens, and PCBs. However, as aforementioned, Fe concentration is considerably decreasing over time; thereby it is not pertinent to classify Fe as a target for recovery. Anyway, for recycling routes that aim to maximize mass recovery, the separation of ferrous materials is indicated as pertinent. Indeed, many recycling routes apply magnetic separation steps to select such pieces [48].

[E13d14e5f88] section=Mass fraction
Considering the mass distribution into components (Table 3), there is a similarity among all components concerning the significant presence of Al and Cu (exception for the relatively low concentration of Al in PCBs, with 1.41%). A distinction can be noted for Co, which is present, in significant concentrations, only in batteries.

[E5fc881c497] section=Economic potential
The analysis of the economic potential took into account the market value and the mass fraction of the materials (Eq. 1). Complete results of EV (economic values) are shown in Supplementary Material for each element and component. A summary of the results is shown in Fig. 2, in the base of 1000 kg for each component and entire devices.

[Ee51af1a7ad] section=Economic potential
For the EV of entire smartphones, Au represents 59% of the total economic potential and is two times higher than the second most valuable material (Pd) (USD 42,415.02 and USD 23,247.13, respectively). In general, Au and Pd are the most valuable elements for both the components casings, screens, and PCBs. In batteries, Co is the element with the highest EV (78% of the total EV of the component), and represents an exception in relation to Au and Pd. Batteries are the only component with a significant presence of Co and no contribution of Au and Pd for the economic potential (Table 3 and Fig. 2). To summarize, priority should be given to Au in all components (after Au, priority to Pd), with an exception for batteries, which have priority for Co.

[E070330707b] section=Economic potential
Analyzing other materials (values can be checked in Supplementary Material), the low relevance of the element Ag draws attention, as it is commonly defined as a target for recycling routes due to its market value. In addition to the elements Au and Pd, the elements Cu, Li, Al, Ni, and Sn are indicated to recycling routes that aim to maximize economic recovery. The summation of the values indicates that 1000 kg of smartphones potentially contains USD 72,202.79 in metals. The most valuable components are PCBs and casings, and the least valuable component is batteries. As discussed for the mass fraction, casings contain significant concentrations of valuable elements, and may not be wasted. By segregating and not processing the casings, 30.6% of the total economic potential is wasted. And, only considering PCBs, 51.6% of the total economic potential would be wasted.

[Ed7ca7ef780] section=Scarcity
In Supplementary Material, the complete resultant values of SRs, from Eq. 2, are provided. Figure 3 shows a summary of results for each component and entire devices.

[E6de72a144e] section=Scarcity
Pd has the highest values of SR for entire smartphones and for the components PCBs, screens and casings (the recovery of Pd represents 66% of the total scarcity index). For batteries, Co has the highest SR value. As Co was already defined as a target through the parameters of mass fraction and economic potential, it is pertinent to include Li as a target for batteries (second material in economic potential and scarcity). To summarize, batteries are the component with distinct targets again (Co and Li), and Pd is defined as target for the other components. For routes that aim to maximize the recovery of scarcity, Au and magnets (elements Nd, Pr and Dy) are also indicated.

[Eababddafca] section=Scarcity
Analyzing the summation of SRs, casings and PCBs are the components with the highest scarcity index. This is due to the high concentration of Au and Pd. In the case of casings, these elements remain adhered to the electronic parts when dismantling the devices (Fig. 1). By recovering Au, Pd, Co, and Li, 90.2% of the total scarcity index is addressed.

[Efa8db932e7] section=Environmental burden as global warming potential
Materials with high EB(GWP) in the cradle to gate approach should be considered for recycling to especially minimize the need of primary processing treatment. The complete results of Eq. 3 are provided in Supplementary Material. A summary for the main elements for each component and entire devices is shown in Fig. 4. It is important to highlight that the resultant values from Eq. 3 do not represent real absolute values of emissions per component and elements, and are only applicable to relative comparisons.

[Efd93eaad1e] section=Environmental burden as global warming potential
The elements Au, Pd, and Al (71%, 9.7 and 8.9%, respectively, for entire devices) had the highest absolute values among all elements. In general, the precious metals group (PMG) present higher environmental impact indexes than other groups of metals for many categories [34].

[Ee2347dbee7] section=Environmental burden as global warming potential
Au is the most influent for screens, casings, and PCBs. The exception is batteries, where Au is absent, and the most influent elements are Co and Al, with 54.5 and 30.8% of the total, respectively. Taking into account only metals in the cradle to gate steps, PCBs are the component with the highest value of GWP, and batteries the lowest. For example, PCBs has GWP value 7.9 times higher than batteries.

[E5c00891811] section=Final interpretation on target components and materials
According to the results and discussions presented in the above sections, it is pertinent to propose two recycling routes: the first with treatment of entire devices, and the second with distinct flows for batteries and the other components separated as the first step of the recycling route. For casings, screens, and PCBs, Cu, Al, Au, and Pd are the main target elements. For batteries, Cu, Al, Co, and Li are the main targets. As discussed, other elements can be included in the recycling routes to maximize recovery rates, with emphasis on Nd, which is relevant for scarcity reasons, Sn and Ni for economic reasons, and Fe for the mass relevance. Figure 5 summarizes the definition of target components and materials. The main point to be highlighted for both routes is that screens and casings could be processed together with PCBs to avoid wasting of relevant materials, and batteries need a diferentiated approach because they have distinct target materials.

[E297096e13c] section=Final interpretation on target components and materials
A route for entire smartphones (route 1) is recommended because, as verified in "Characterization" section, the current devices’ designs are obstructing an efficient manual dismantling process by recyclers. A route for entire smartphones means a route that avoid initial steps of manual segregation of components, being the entire smartphone directly processed by means of mechanical, hydro or pyro techniques. This approach is a gap in literature and may generate interesting results. The importance of considering all components, and minimizing initial steps of manua treatment is to prevent material losses and facilitating the work of small and medium recyclers worldwide that allocate a lot of manpower to perform this activity.

[E1c7e8b6628] section=Final interpretation on target components and materials
A route manually separating batteries from the other components from the beginning, and jointly processing casings, PCBs, and screens is indicated to maximize the recovery of the target elements in the terms of this study (route 2). An exclusive recycling route for batteries must be considered as they have distinct targets materials for both criteria of mass fraction, economic potential, scarcity, and environmental burden, and an exclusive route for them may be important to maximize and prioritize the recovery of these targets (Co and Li). For the other components (casings, PCBs, and screens), similar target materials were defined, and, in the point of view of the analysis proposed in this study, they must be processed together to avoid material losses.

[E31a1657176] section=Final interpretation on target components and materials
It is important to note that, for real processes, the recovery of an element is limited by the recycling techniques selected and thermodynamic features involved (this limitation is a challenge mainly for the route 1). Taken into consideration that Cu, Li, Au, Pd, Al, and Co are targets for recovery, it is also essential to select recycling strategies that ideally promote the valorization of all of them. The study of Nakajima et al. [49] explored the distribution of the elements in the smelting process of metallic materials (main recycling strategies worldwide considering e-waste) and presented limitations, impurities, and losses for each case. For example, if entire smartphone scrap is directly sent for the copper smelting process, Cu, Au and Pd can be adequately valorized. However, Al, Li, and Co would be lost in the slag, thus requiring pretreatment steps to separate them before the smelting process or considering other recycling strategies. It implies that the separation of components/materials (particularly the batteries/Li and Co) is essential to promote an adequate valorization of the residues even after processing entire smartphones in route 1.

[E1d71216e22] section=Final interpretation on target components and materials
Finally, trough the recovery of the target elements, there would be a potential benefit of USD 71,383.78 from 1000 kg of smartphones (98.9% of the total economic potential). The recovery of scarce metals could reach the SR value of 11.537, which means 90.2% of the total scarcity evaluated by this methodology. Regarding the EB (GWP), the recovery of the target metals addresses 96.7% of total impact. These target elements represent 72.9% of the total mass of metals, and, incrementing a possible recycling of polymers, 62.4% of the total mass of the smartphones could be addressed.

[E75c3d16018] section=Conclusions
Particularly for the characterization, at the date of realization, this study was the second more comprehensive in the number of elements, the most representative in the number of devices and samples, and the first to characterize separately all components. In terms of objectives, the work undertaken has contributed to filling gaps in the literature concerning the determination of the composition of smartphones, encompassing their various components and trends. It serves as a source of relevant and reliable information for recyclers and researchers. The comprehensive characterization has also facilitated the development of a supportive methodology for identifying the key elements for recovery. As the composition of REEE varies over time (being a limitation), it is suggested that similar studies be undertaken to keep the data and conclusions up-to-date.

[E26a7e05e5f] section=Conclusions
In the manual dismantling of devices, it was noted that newer devices are harder to dismantle, as there is no access to the interior of the devices. The design of the newer devices is not in agreement with the current ecodesign recommendations to facilitate the replacement of components and recycling processes. Eco-design initiatives urges to be indeed applied by the manufacturers and future studies in this regard are suggested. About the elementary composition, although the limitation of only two groups of different manufacturing years were tested, significant and consolidated variations were found, such as the increase in the concentration of Al, and the decrease in the concentrations of some precious and critical metals (Au, Nd, Pr, V…), which are in agreement with previous literature. As an environmentally positive point, the elements Pb and PMG were found to significantly decrease over time. Screens, PCBs, and batteries of newer devices had concentrations of Pb below the LOQ in their composition.

[E7931ae51e6] section=Conclusions
These variations of composition over time may impose regular updates to real recycling routes (including in the choice of materials they recover) to remain up to date in their processes and with adequate efficiencies. The methodology for definition of target components and materials, resulted in the selection of Cu, Al, Au, Li, Co, and Pd as the main targets. The definition included important parameters, namely mass fraction, economic potential, scarcity, and environmental burden, in a way to possibly the maximization of the recovery of these parameters. Circular economy seeks optimization in resource usage, with reduced dependence on primary raw materials, and all the proposed parameters are important to be taken into account. With a focus on scarcity and environmental burden parameters, our society should explore alternative and renewable sources of materials to minimize the environmental impact throughout the products life cycle. Recycling materials considered scarce and with a significant environmental footprint is crucial in this regard. It is important to mention that this methodology can be applied for any residue and is flexible to specific circumstances and contexts, being an opportunity for future studies. As a limitation, this definition depends on input data that are dificult to evaluate and access, and not available worldwide (for example, supply risk indexes for different regions and reliable data on environmental burden). In the end, two recycling routes are suggested for future studies: the first with entire smartphones (without initial dismantling), and the second divided into two flows, only separating batteries (because of the different target materials: Li and Co).
