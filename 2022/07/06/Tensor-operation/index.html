Tensor Operation

In machine learning/big data we can think of a tensor as an nD-array. The picture below is an example of a rank 3 tensor with size (3,4,2).

Tensor unfolding

Unfolding a tensor to a matrix (“matrization”) is a
fundamental operation for most tensor methods and we can do it in different ways (use the tensor above as example).

Mode-1 unfolding: The column vectors of ana_nan​ are column vectors of A1A_1A1​

Tensor-matrix multiplication

1-mode multiplication: U⋅anU \cdot a_nU⋅an​ (UUU is the matrix that we need to multiply, and ana_nan​ is the tensor we use)

(1) Mode-1 unfolding the tensor an→A1a_n \rightarrow A_1an​→A1​

(2) Matrix-matrix multiplication U⋅A1=Y1U \cdot A_{1} = Y_{1}U⋅A1​=Y1​

(3) Refold (fold the matrix back to a tensor) Y1→y1Y_{1} \rightarrow y_{1}Y1​→y1​

Same principle for mode-2 multiplication and mode-3 multiplication etc.

Outer product

Outer product between two vectors a1,a2a^{1}, a^{2}a1,a2 is a1⋅a2⊤a^{1} \cdot a^{2^{\top}}a1⋅a2⊤, which is a 2D-matrix of
rank=1 .

Outer product between three vectors a1,a2,a3a^{1}, a^{2}, a^{3}a1,a2,a3 is a tensor ana_nan​ with three slices, and each slice is of rank=1. Each element in the tensor: an(i,j,k)a_n(i,j,k)an​(i,j,k) is defined by a1i⋅a2j⋅a3k{a^{1}}_{i} \cdot {a^{2}}_{j} \cdot {a^{3}}_{k}a1i​⋅a2j​⋅a3k​ (iii means i-th slice, jjj means j-th row, kkk means k-th column).

Frontal slices: in a 3∗3∗33*3*33∗3∗3 tensor, the frontal slices is tensor[n, n, n], and n can be :, 0, 1, 2. : means choose all, 0 means to choose the first one, 1 means to choose the second one, and 2 means to choose the third one. The first n represents slice, the second n represents row, and the last n represents column.
