QR Decomposition

The QR (orthogonal triangular) decomposition is the most effective and widely used method to find all the eigenvalues of a general matrix. It decomposes the matrix into a normal orthogonal matrix QQQ and an upper triangular matrix RRR, so it is called the QR decomposition. There are two common methods of QR decomposition, one is based on Gram-Schmidt, and the other one is based on Householder reflectors.

QR decomposition based on Gram-Schmidt

Let say a matrix A=(a1a2a3)A=(a_{1} \quad a_{2} \quad a_{3})A=(a1​a2​a3​) for example, then Ax=(a1a2a3)x=bAx=(a_{1} \quad a_{2} \quad a_{3})x=bAx=(a1​a2​a3​)x=b. To find the QQQ and RRR for AAA, we need to orthogonalize and normalize every vector in AAA (* for the first vector a1a_1a1​, only normalization is required).

(1) Normalize a1a_1a1​: g1=a1∥a1∥,a1=∥a1∥g1\quad g_{1}=\frac{a_{1}}{\parallel a_{1} \parallel}, \quad a_{1}=\left\|a_{1}\right\| g_{1}g1​=∥a1​∥a1​​,a1​=∥a1​∥g1​

(2) Orthogonalize a2a_2a2​: a2′=a2−(a2⊤g1)g1\quad a_{2}^{\prime}=a_{2}-(a_{2}^{\top}g_{1})g_{1}a2′​=a2​−(a2⊤​g1​)g1​

(3) Normalize a2a_2a2​: g2=a2′∥a2′∥,a2=(a2⊤g1)g1+∥a2′∥g2\quad g_{2}=\frac{a_{2}^{\prime}}{\left\|a_{2}^{\prime}\right\|}, \quad a_{2}=\left(a_{2}^{\top} g_{1}\right) g_{1}+\left\|a_{2}^{\prime}\right\| g_{2}g2​=∥a2′​∥a2′​​,a2​=(a2⊤​g1​)g1​+∥a2′​∥g2​

(4) Orthogonalise a3a_{3}a3​: a3′=a3−(a3⊤g1)g1−(a3⊤g2)g2\quad a_{3}^{\prime}=a_{3}-\left(a_{3}^{\top} g_{1}\right) g_{1}-\left(a_{3}^{\top} g_{2}\right) g_{2}a3′​=a3​−(a3⊤​g1​)g1​−(a3⊤​g2​)g2​

One problem with Gram-Schmidt is that g1,g2,g3g_{1}, g_{2}, g_{3}g1​,g2​,g3​ won’t be exactly orthogonal due to error in the computations (round-off).

And as g3g_3g3​ depends on g1g_1g1​ and g2g_2g2​, the problem tends to get worse and worse. The vectors get less and less orthogonal in practice. Therefore, it’s better to use Householder reflectors when we apply QR decomposition.
